## Part B — The Job engine

### Locked here (Phase 4 — the one-way doors; numbering continues Phase 2's #13–20)

Each is enforced by the database, the store layer or a named gate, never by convention; the admin console (P7) is a parallel write path and passes through the same store functions and triggers.

21. **The lifecycle is a table, enforced by a trigger.** States: `draft`, `previewed`, `confirmed`, `scheduled`, `running`, `paused`, `cancelling`, `completing`, and the terminal `succeeded`, `cancelled`, `expired`, `failed`. (`cancelling` is kept from Phase 1: an item in flight finishes after a cancel and is reported once, so a cancel is not instantaneous.) The legal `(from, to)` pairs are rows of `platform.job_transitions` (with which actors may take each: `user`, `engine`, `admin`); `jobs_transition_guard` refuses any other change of `status` (SQLSTATE 23001, constraint `job_transition_illegal`); terminal states stay final (`jobs_terminal_is_final`, Phase 2). **Every transition writes a `platform.job_events` row** (who: kind and id, when: the database's clock, why: a non-blank reason) by trigger from three transaction-local settings the store sets (`platform.actor_kind`, `platform.actor_id`, `platform.reason`); a status change without them is refused (`job_transition_needs_an_actor`). `job_events` is append-only for everyone.
22. **The one-active-job slot covers every state in which credits are held or a module may be working**: `confirmed`, `scheduled`, `running`, `paused`, `cancelling`, `completing` (the partial unique index replaces `jobs_one_active_per_user_module`). Amends #14.
23. **A reserve is whole, dearest and once, over the units a job can actually reach** (amends #17). `platform.unit_costs` gains `reachable`; the price snapshot holds exactly the reachable units of the module and the reserve rate is the dearest of those. Module 1's vendor unit `m1_vendor` is reachable only when the owner has switched the vendor fallback on (`PLATFORM_M1_VENDOR_REACHABLE`, mirrored from module 1's own configuration and declared in `OWNER-ACTIONS.md` as a pair with module 1's `TM_EXTERNAL_*` dials); with it off the reserve is at 1 per row and not 2, and **a charge for an unreachable unit cannot be written** (the charge row's composite foreign key to the snapshot has no such unit; a vendor answer while the unit is off is flagged, never charged, and raises a job event). Both directions fail safe for the customer.
24. **Confirm is one transaction**: the job row locked, the stale-preview check, the suppression re-application, the slot (the index), the price snapshot and the reserve (`money.ReserveIn`), the transition to `confirmed` or `scheduled`, and the events. It is idempotent per client key (`platform.jobs.confirm_key`, unique per user). A balance that does not cover the whole list is refused with the shortfall; a second job for the same user and module is refused by the index and leaves **no partial reserve** (the whole transaction rolls back).
25. **A job's rows and results are the platform's own and forever.** `platform.job_rows` holds the list (one row per input line, classified once); `platform.job_results` holds **every row's outcome**, including the platform's own (`malformed`, `duplicate`, `suppressed`, `excluded`), written idempotently by `(job_id, item_ref)` in the same transaction as the settlement it explains, never updated, never deleted. **`item_ref` is `job_rows.id`** for every module (module 1's `item_id`, modules 2 and 3's `job_item_id`). Ledger charges, `job_results` and the module's report are equal three ways (a proof query, `platform.job_settlement_drift`, and a test per module).
26. **What a module can do to a running job is data, and an adapter must do what the data says.** `platform.modules` gains `can_pause` and `can_cancel` (seeded from the module code, see "Findings"); the database refuses `running → paused` unless the module can pause, and `running → cancelling` unless it can cancel. Pause is **never faked by cancel-and-resubmit** (a duplicate-send risk).
27. **The engine never invents an outcome.** A module that does not answer leaves the job where it is; a job with no progress for the module's staleness dial raises a `stale` event (and stays running); a job is `succeeded` only when the module reports every item final and every item is recorded; an item the module cannot be seen to have settled is free and flagged, never charged (rule 20).
28. **Every job rule is enforced where every writer passes.** The job store functions take the acting `user_id` from the authenticated principal, never from a request body or path; every statement is scoped by it; the engine's loops are the only code that submits to a module; `platform.job_rows`, `job_results`, `job_events` and `job_transitions` are written only through `internal/jobs` (a gate in the style of `check-user-lists`, Part B chunk B6).
29. **The API authenticates Supabase access tokens** (`Authorization: Bearer`): signature (HS256 secret, or an asymmetric key from the JWKS endpoint), `exp`, `nbf`, `iss`, `aud` (`authenticated` by default), the `sub` resolved through `tenant.Resolver.ByAuthUser` (an inactive or unknown login is refused). Nothing but the verified `sub` names the user.
30. **CSV that opens in a spreadsheet is safe and still exact.** Every cell is written with `encoding/csv`; a cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, TAB, CR or `'` gets one leading `'`. The inverse is "strip one leading `'` when present", so the mapping is injective (a property test over arbitrary strings proves the round trip) and no cell starts with a formula trigger.

### What is built (design)

**Tables (migrations `platform_0011`…`0015`, each with a down).**
- `0011 lifecycle`: status vocabulary, `job_transitions` (seeded), `job_events`, the transition guard and event triggers, the new slot index, `jobs` columns (`confirm_key`, `preview` jsonb, `preview_fingerprint`, `previewed_at`, `confirmed_at`, `started_at`, `finished_at`, `start_deadline`, `last_progress_at`, `stale_alerted_at`, `cancel_requested_at`), `jobs_guard` amended (`frozen_settings` and `preview` change only while `draft` or `previewed`), `platform.modules.can_pause/can_cancel`.
- `0012 rows and results`: `job_rows(id, job_id, ordinal, email_raw, email_norm, state, reason, fields jsonb, submitted_at, UNIQUE(job_id, ordinal))`, `job_results(job_id, item_ref → job_rows.id, ordinal, email_norm, module_state, reason, verdict, class, unit, settle_kind, flags jsonb, settled_at, PK(job_id, item_ref))`, the proof view `job_settlement_drift`, grants (the roles hold what each needs, never UPDATE/DELETE on `job_results`), `tests/harness/grants_test.go` classes.
- `0013 reachable units`: `unit_costs.reachable` (+ `reachable_source`, `reachable_checked_at`), `platform.set_unit_reachable(unit, reachable, source)` (SECURITY DEFINER, audited into `unit_cost_history`), `job_unit_prices` refuses an unreachable unit, `ledger_check_reserve` amended, the seeded state (`m1_vendor` unreachable).
- `0014 rendering hook`: nothing structural for P5 beyond `job_rows.fields`; the P4 renderer is the identity (`internal/jobs.PlainRenderer`) behind an interface P5 replaces, so a send job's `public.job_items` rows carry the campaign's subject and body as given, and the missing-field count in the preview is 0 until P5.
- `0014 suppressed flag` (close-out D2): `job_rows.suppressed` and `job_results.suppressed`. `0015 engine schedule` (close-out D8, D9): `next_poll_at`, `idle_polls`, `items_ensured_at` and the guard that clears a backoff on any status change. Module 1's caller key is provisioned NOT by a migration but by the install step `db/install/postmodule1.sql` (chunk B16).

**Code.** `internal/jobs` (the store: draft, rows, preview, confirm, pause/resume/cancel requests, results reads, events; the only writer of the job tables), `internal/jobengine` (the leader-only loops: starter, poller/settler, completer, expirer, staleness, repair), `internal/faultpoint` (inert unless built with the tag `faultpoints`), `internal/suppress` (A2), `internal/auth` (token verification), `internal/api` (the job routes), `internal/csvsafe`, `internal/leader` (the leader session extracted from `internal/entitlements` so one lock serves both services), config dials (each with its consumer at the seeding commit, `Docs/config-reference.md`).

**The loops.** One leader (the existing session lock, key 7_200_000_002); every step is also idempotent and serialised per (user, module) by the money advisory lock, so a deposed leader finishing a step and a new leader starting it cannot double a submit or a settle.
1. **Starter**: `confirmed`/`scheduled` jobs whose time has come. Inserts the module-facing rows (modules 2 and 3: `public.job_items` with `id = job_rows.id`, `campaign_id = jobs.id`, the rendered subject, body and plain text, idempotent by id; module 1: the plan), submits (module 1: `Submit(plan, key = job id)`, replay-safe; modules 2 and 3: `Run(job_item_id)` per row, replay-safe, with `submitted_at` recorded in batches), then `→ running`. A module that is down or refuses (`Paused`, `Unavailable`, the 409 backpressure) leaves the job where it is; the retry stops at `start_deadline = coalesce(scheduled_start, confirmed_at) + max start delay`, after which the job is `expired` and its reserve released (charge row P-02, free). A module that refuses the job for good fails it (`failed`, rows without a result P-04, reserve released) only while it holds none of its rows; once it holds any (rows stamped as submitted, or a job already `running`) the engine does not fail it, because the module goes on sending the rows it holds and they must still be recorded and charged: it writes one `flag` job event (the module's code word only), keeps observing, and the job goes stale like any job that cannot go on. Module 1's own id of a job is `platform.jobs.module_job_ref`, written by the move to `running` and read back with the job (`jobs.EngineJob.ModuleRef`); a module 1 job whose reference is not on the job waits.
2. **Poller/settler**: for `running`/`cancelling`/`completing` jobs, every tick reads the module's status cheaply; when it shows terminal items not yet recorded (and at most every settle interval), walks the report and records every terminal item through `money.Settle` plus `job_results` in one transaction per page (idempotent); updates `last_progress_at`; raises `stale` events.
3. **Completer**: when the module reports every item final and every row has a result, one transaction moves `running|cancelling → completing → succeeded` (or `cancelled`), then `money.ReleaseIn` returns the remainder. A **repair pass** releases any terminal job whose reserve still has a remainder (a crash window, or an admin who ended a job).
4. **Faultpoints** (tag `faultpoints`, never in the production image): `confirm.before_commit`, `submit.after_module`, `start.before_running`, `settle.after_commit`, `release.mid_tx`; hit by name and count from `PLATFORM_FAULTPOINT`, they `SIGKILL` the process itself. The harness builds its platform image with the tag; a gate proves the default build contains none (`scripts/check-faultpoints.sh`).

**API (Go, authenticated; P6 builds only UI on it).** `POST /v1/jobs` (create a draft with the list: JSON rows or CSV, module, settings, optional `source_job_id`, scheduled start), `GET /v1/jobs`, `GET /v1/jobs/{id}`, `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/preview`, `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/confirm` (`Idempotency-Key`; body names the preview fingerprint and the `unknown` choice for a chained send), `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/pause`, `/resume`, `/cancel` (typed refusals where the module cannot), `GET /v1/jobs/{id}/events`, `GET /v1/jobs/{id}/results?cursor=` (paginated), `GET /v1/jobs/{id}/results.csv` (streamed). Errors use the platform envelope; a job of another user is `404`.

**Preview.** For a list and a module: rows, malformed (the `norm.Validate` reason), duplicates (normalised), suppressed (per user, `internal/suppress.Filter`), missing-field count (0 until P5), per-verdict counts when chained (from the source job's `job_results`), **the exact credits to reserve** (rows that will reach the module × the reserve rate over the reachable units), the shortfall, and a fingerprint (rows, prices of the reachable units, reachability, the user's suppression state, the source job's results, the chain choice). Confirm recomputes the fingerprint under the lock and refuses a stale one (`409 preview_stale`).

**Chaining.** A send job may name a `succeeded` verify job of the same user as `source_job_id`. The rows are the source's results by verdict: default exclude `invalid`, include `valid` and `risky`, the user's choice for `unknown` (**no default**, owner ruling OD-4: a source with any `unknown` result needs an explicit include or exclude at confirm, equal to the one the job was previewed under); excluded rows are `job_results` of the platform class `excluded`, free. Confirm checks the balance of the send module (reserve for the selected rows) and, for module 3, the entitlement and the module's feasibility; the selected verdicts and only they are submitted, proven item by item.

**Module 3 pre-checks at confirm**: the entitlement is enabled and not expired (`platform.m3_entitlements`), the user is not suspended (the observed suspension), and the module's `FeasibilityForCampaign` names no cause; a module that cannot be reached refuses an immediate confirm with `module_unavailable` (no credits are held for a job that cannot start; a scheduled job is not pre-checked against availability, only against the entitlement).

### Findings: what "pause" can honestly mean per module (derived from module code)

- **Module 1 (verify)**: the served surface is `POST /v1/verify`, `POST /v1/verify/bulk`, `GET /v1/jobs/{id}`, `GET /v1/jobs/{id}/results`, `GET /v1/describe`, health and readiness (`control-plane/internal/api/api.go`, `Routes()`); **no cancel and no pause of a job**. The only "paused" the module knows is its operator front-door kill switch (`processing_paused` on a job, module-wide, not the caller's choice; `api.go` kill-switch ladder). A submitted job is worked by the module until every row settles.
- **Module 2 (send-managed)**: `quote`, `run`, `report`, `describe`, **`cancel`** (`platformapi/method_dispatch.go`): a cancel makes every unclaimed item `cancelled` and an item in flight finishes. `dispatch_paused` in the report is the operator's dispatch rung, global to the module and not per campaign.
- **Module 3 (send-byo)**: the same five routes with a cancel since the close-out. Its per-user **suspension** (an admin route) halts new claims for the whole user and is owned by the platform's entitlement reconciler (`platform.m3_sync`: a job pause that used it would be undone by the next reconcile, would also gate the user's test button, test send and account creation, and would stop every other campaign of the user); account-level pauses are the module's own health actions (`pause_reason`).
- **Therefore**: no module can stop and later continue **one job**. The seeded capabilities are m1 `can_pause=false, can_cancel=false`; m2 and m3 `can_pause=false, can_cancel=true`. What the platform offers: a **scheduled** job can be paused (nothing was submitted; the start is deferred until resume, the slot and the reserve are held) and resumed; a **running** job cannot be paused or (module 1) cancelled, and the API says so with a typed reason instead of pretending. Options for the owner are in "Open decisions" (1).

## Open decisions (mine to put to you; each has a default that is safe for the customer; none blocks work)

1. **Pause of a running job.** No module can pause one job (Findings). Default: refuse with a typed reason; a scheduled job can be paused. Options: (a) additive module routes (`POST /v1/pause` and `/v1/resume` per campaign for modules 2 and 3: claims stop, queued items stay queued; `POST /v1/cancel` for module 1), the platform flips the seeded capability and the adapter calls them; (b) platform-side batching (submit a large list in batches and pause between them): honest at batch granularity but a campaign per batch fights the one-active-campaign rule of module 2; (c) leave it refused. Never cancel-and-resubmit.
2. **Cancelling a running module 1 job.** Module 1 has no cancel. Default: refused (`cancel_not_supported`): the customer is charged for verification they asked for and cannot stop. Option: ask for an additive module 1 cancel (pending rows become cancelled and are free).
3. **Suppressed rows in a verify job.** Default (as written in the brief): suppression is applied to every job, so a suppressed address is not verified and not charged and is shown as `suppressed` in the results. Option: apply it to send jobs only. **RULED (OD-3): send jobs only.** In a verify job a suppressed address stays `pending`, is verified and charged like any other row and carries `suppressed` in its results (`platform_0014`: `job_rows.suppressed`, `job_results.suppressed`, derived from the row by the database); a chained send still excludes it at the send's own confirm.
4. **The `unknown` choice of a chained send** defaulted to **exclude**. **RULED (OD-4): explicit, never defaulted.** With at least one `unknown` result in the source the confirm must carry include or exclude (`422 unknown_choice_required`) and the choice must be the one the job was previewed under (`409 preview_stale`, reason `unknown_choice_changed`); with none, no choice is needed.
5. **Rendering.** P4 sends the campaign's subject and body as given (identity renderer); merge fields, spintax and the plain-text generation are P5 behind the same interface.
6. **List size.** A verify job is limited to 100,000 rows (module 1's limit); a larger list is refused at creation with the limit named. Larger lists are P8 (load) work.
7. **Module 1's vendor reachability** is the platform dial `PLATFORM_M1_VENDOR_REACHABLE` (default off). Option: an additive field in module 1's `describe` the platform mirrors and checks the dial against.
8. **The dials** (all documented, each with its consumer): tick 1.5 s, settle interval 10 s, staleness per module (module 1 30 min, modules 2 and 3 15 min), max start delay 30 min, max rows per job 100,000, results page 1,000.
